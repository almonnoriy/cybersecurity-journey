# Information Security

## 1. Definition of Information Security

Information security is the practice of protecting information from unauthorised access, disclosure, modification, loss or destruction.

It applies to information in all forms, including digital files, physical documents and verbal communications. Its purpose is to ensure that information remains accessible to authorised individuals, accurate and available when needed.

### 1.1 Importance of Information Security

Information security is important to organisations for several reasons:

- **Protection of sensitive information:** Safeguards customer, employee and organisational information against unauthorised disclosure.
- **Financial protection:** Helps reduce financial losses associated with fraud, ransomware and security breaches.
- **Trust and reputation:** Protects the confidence customers, employees and stakeholders place in an organisation.
- **Legal compliance:** Helps organisations meet applicable data protection requirements, including the Nigeria Data Protection Act (NDPA) 2023.

### 1.2 Information Security vs Cybersecurity

Information security and cybersecurity are related disciplines, but they differ in scope.

| Information Security | Cybersecurity |
|---|---|
| Protects information in all forms, including physical, digital and verbal information. | Focuses on protecting digital systems, networks, devices and information. |
| Covers physical documents, digital records and verbal communications. | Covers computer systems, applications, networks and digital environments. |
| Example: Keeping student records in a secured filing cabinet. | Example: Protecting a university student portal using a firewall and multi-factor authentication. |

Information security has a broader scope, while cybersecurity focuses specifically on digital environments.

## 2. Information Assets

An information asset is any information, resource or system that has value to an organisation and requires protection.

Information assets may include databases, documents, software applications, records and other resources that store, process or manage valuable information.

### 2.1 Examples of Information Assets in a University

| Information Asset | Importance of Protection |
|---|---|
| Student records | Contains personal information and academic records that must remain confidential and accurate. |
| Examination papers and results | Requires protection against unauthorised disclosure and modification to maintain academic integrity. |
| Staff records and payroll | Contains sensitive employee and financial information. |
| Research data and intellectual property | Protects valuable research findings and academic work from theft, loss or unauthorised disclosure. |
| Learning management systems and network infrastructure | Supports teaching, communication and administrative activities across the university. |

## 3. Data Classification

Data classification is the process of organising information into categories according to its sensitivity and the level of protection it requires.

It helps organisations determine who should access particular information and what security measures should be applied.

### 3.1 Levels of Data Classification

**1. Public**

Information that can be accessed by anyone without causing significant harm to the organisation.

Example: University prospectuses and publicly available course information.

**2. Internal**

Information intended for use within an organisation and not generally meant for public distribution.

Example: Internal staff directories and administrative memos.

**3. Confidential**

Sensitive information that should only be accessed by authorised individuals. Unauthorised disclosure could cause harm to individuals or the organisation.

Example: Student personal records, contact details and academic information.

**4. Restricted**

Highly sensitive information that requires strict access controls. Unauthorised disclosure could result in serious consequences.

Example: Examination papers before an examination and sensitive staff banking information.

## 4. Three States of Data

Data exists in three primary states: data at rest, data in transit and data in use. Each state requires appropriate protection.

### 4.1 Data at Rest

Data at rest refers to information stored on a device or storage system that is not currently being transferred or actively processed.

**Examples:**
- Student records stored in a database.
- Backup files stored on an external hard drive.

**Protection measures:**
- Encryption of stored information.
- Access controls.
- Secure storage and backup management.

### 4.2 Data in Transit

Data in transit refers to information being transferred between devices, systems or networks.

**Examples:**
- A student logging into a university portal.
- An email containing examination results being sent to an authorised recipient.

**Protection measures:**
- HTTPS and TLS encryption.
- Secure file transfer methods.
- VPN connections where appropriate.

### 4.3 Data in Use

Data in use refers to information that is actively being accessed, processed or modified by a user or application.

**Examples:**
- A lecturer editing examination results in a spreadsheet.
- An administrator viewing student records.

**Protection measures:**
- Multi-factor authentication.
- Role-based access control.
- Automatic screen locking and session timeouts.

## 5. Information Security in a University Environment

Universities handle different categories of information, including student records, examination results, staff information and financial records. These assets require appropriate classification and security controls.

### 5.1 Classification of University Information Assets

| Information Asset | Classification | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| Staff directory | Internal | Intended primarily for use within the university. |
| Student information | Confidential | Contains personal information that should only be available to authorised individuals. |
| Examination results | Restricted | Unauthorised disclosure or modification could affect academic integrity. |
| Financial information | Restricted | Exposure could lead to financial fraud and privacy violations. |

### 5.2 Consequences of Unauthorised Access

Unauthorised access to university information systems and assets can result in:

- **Identity theft and financial fraud:** Stolen personal and banking information may be used for impersonation or fraudulent activities.
- **Reputational damage and legal consequences:** Security breaches can reduce public trust and expose the institution to regulatory penalties under applicable data protection laws.

### 5.3 Recommended Security Measures

**Access Control and Multi-Factor Authentication**

Access should be granted according to users' responsibilities. The principle of least privilege ensures that individuals only receive the access necessary to perform their duties. Multi-factor authentication provides an additional layer of account protection.

**Encryption**

Encryption helps protect information stored in databases and backup systems, as well as information transmitted across networks.

**Security Awareness and Backups**

Security awareness training helps staff and students recognise threats such as phishing. Regular and tested backups help organisations restore information and services following data loss or security incidents.

---

## 6. Conclusion

Information security is essential for protecting valuable organisational information against unauthorised access, disclosure, modification and loss.

Understanding information assets, data classification and the three states of data helps organisations identify their security requirements and apply appropriate protective measures.
